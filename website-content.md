### 🚨 CONTENT GENERATION RULES (CRITICAL)
Before generating any website content in the following steps, you MUST adhere to these strict formatting rules for EVERY section:

1. **Visual Heading Structure:** Every section MUST start with a short eyebrow label placed above the larger main heading.
2. **Eyebrow Label:** The eyebrow must be one or two words only (for example, "Local Care", "Clear Pricing", or "Real Proof"). It is a visual label, not necessarily a semantic HTML heading. This prevents an H3 from appearing before its parent H2 in the final implementation.
3. **Main Heading:** The main section heading must be a clear, customer-facing H2. It must describe the point of the section in natural language. Do NOT use meta-labels such as "Hero Section", "Trust Section", "Problem, Symptoms and Need", or "About Us Section".
4. **H1 Rule:** Use one primary keyword or a clear brand/service phrase in the H1. Do not force multiple keywords into the H1 or repeat keywords unnaturally across the page.
5. **Finished Copy Only:** Write the actual website copy. Do not give instructions to a writer, designer or developer, and do not leave template wording in the deliverable.
6. **Short Paragraphs:** Keep paragraphs to one or two sentences. Use bullets where they make decisions, inclusions, symptoms, steps, pricing or proof easier to scan.
7. **Hero Copy:** Use one short supporting paragraph below the hero heading. Aim for fewer than 30 words, followed by reassurance points and clear calls to action.
8. **Total Word Count:** The entire generated page content must be strictly between 900 and 1,200 words unless the user gives a different range.

**Example of correct section formatting:**
Eyebrow: Local Care
## Trusted Car Service in Glen Eden
[Short 1-2 sentence paragraph or bullet points go here...]

### 🚨 INTERNAL LINKING RULES (CRITICAL)

1. Add internal links as part of the content, not as an afterthought.
2. Use descriptive anchor text that tells the reader where the link leads. Never use "click here".
3. Link each service card or service mention to the relevant parent or child service page.
4. Link parent service pages to their key child service pages and link related services where that helps the customer solve the next problem.
5. Link high-intent sections to the contact page, quote form or booking page with an action-led CTA.
6. Link to About, reviews, gallery, pricing or FAQ pages only when they support the current decision.
7. Use site-relative URLs in content drafts, for example `[Hybrid Battery Repair](/hybrid-repair/hybrid-battery-repair)`. Convert them to the live absolute domain only after the domain is confirmed.
8. Do not add links simply to increase link count. Every link must be useful to the reader and fit naturally in the sentence or card.

### 🚨 PRICING, CLAIMS AND TRUST RULES (CRITICAL)

1. Use "from" or "starts from" for variable service pricing unless a fixed price is confirmed.
2. State tax, inclusions, exclusions and quote conditions exactly as supplied. Do not invent terms or apply a tax treatment that has not been confirmed.
3. Explain the practical factors that affect the final quote where relevant, such as the make, model, fault, parts, size or scope of work.
4. Publish only approved claims. Keep certifications, guarantees, payment options, warranties, review ratings, insurance statements, awards and authority claims out of final copy until they are verified.
5. Use real proof wherever possible: customer reviews, address, owner or team experience, photos, case examples, accreditation, before-and-after evidence and clear process explanations.
6. Do not claim services, outcomes, locations or comparisons that the business cannot substantiate.

### 🚨 SERVICE CARD RULES (CRITICAL)

When a services section will be designed as cards, write each card as a complete, standalone content block:

1. Service name.
2. One- or two-sentence customer-facing description.
3. Two to four practical details, inclusions, symptoms, pricing points or outcomes.
4. A contextual internal link to the relevant service page.

Include every service required by the approved menu or page brief. Do not collapse distinct menu services into one vague paragraph.

### STEP 3 — Generate Comprehensive Outline Structure
Create a detailed outline following these structural points. **(You must apply the 🚨 CONTENT GENERATION RULES from above to every single section)**:

#### Option A: Standard Service & Landing Pages
1. **Hero**: Outcome-focused eyebrow and main heading. Include exactly three high-impact reassurance bullet points and clear call-to-actions.
2. **AI Overview / About**: Eyebrow and main heading optimized for seamless LLM extraction.
3. **Problem / Symptom / Need**: Eyebrow and main heading detailing signs or problems leading to the service.
4. **Service / Product Types**: Card-ready service blocks. Include each relevant service from the approved menu, practical details and contextual internal links to deeper pages.
5. **Mid-Page CTA Banner**: Eyebrow and main heading. Positioned immediately after the services section, with a short message and action-oriented CTA.
6. **Process / How It Works**: Eyebrow and main heading. Step-by-step service explanation using short bullet points.
7. **Benefits, Outcomes, or Advantages**: Eyebrow and main heading. Measurable or trustworthy gains.
8. **Cost / Pricing Section**: Approved starting costs, inclusions and the primary factors affecting the final quote.
9. **Alternatives / Comparisons**: Eyebrow and main heading. Comparison with other solutions.
10. **Aftercare / Maintenance / Usage**: Eyebrow and main heading. Post-service advice.
11. **Trust & E-E-A-T Signals**: Approved local expertise, certifications, awards, expert credentials, proof and process transparency.
12. **Local Context / Service Area**: Eyebrow and main heading. Suburbs, landmarks and regional relevance.
13. **Testimonial Section**: Approved, genuine social proof only.
14. **FAQ Section**: Eyebrow and main heading. Five to ten LLM-optimized questions tightly focused on the target search intent.
15. **CTA Section**: Eyebrow and main heading. Includes an integrated booking, quote or contact action.

#### Option B: Premium Direct-Response Homepage
1. **Hero**: Outcome-focused eyebrow and main heading. Include exactly three high-impact reassurance bullets and clear CTAs.
2. **Trust Section**: Eyebrow and main heading. Short, punchy proof of local focus, business history, credentials, guarantees, payment terms or process confidence.
3. **Who We Are / About Section**: Eyebrow and main heading. Keep this section concise and fact-led. Avoid repeating the hero.
4. **Emotional Concerns / Pain Points**: Eyebrow and main heading. Speak directly to real worries, then reassure the reader with a practical next step.
5. **Services**: Card-ready service blocks with relevant internal links.
6. **Mid-Page CTA**: A concise message and action-led contact, quote or booking CTA.
7. **Benefits / Outcomes**: Practical outcomes that matter to the customer.
8. **Pricing / Value**: Approved starting prices, factors affecting the final quote and value context.
9. **Process**: A clear explanation of what happens after the customer makes contact.
10. **Trust Proof**: Approved reviews, credentials, team experience, real photos, guarantees or payment information.
11. **Local Context**: Relevant local service-area and location proof.
12. **Testimonials**: Genuine, approved customer evidence.
13. **FAQ**: Short questions that remove common barriers to booking.
14. **Final CTA**: A direct booking, quote or call action.

### STEP 4 — Draft, Validate and Finalise
- Draft finished customer-facing copy using the approved outline and the content generation rules above.
- Add internal links, relevant CTAs and approved proof while writing.
- Check every eyebrow is one or two words and appears above its main heading.
- Check the H1 uses one primary keyword naturally.
- Check the hero has one supporting paragraph under 30 words.
- Check that each section has a short paragraph and useful bullets where appropriate.
- Check service content is card-ready and covers every required menu service.
- Check all pricing, tax wording, claims, trust statements and exclusions against the approved source material.
- Remove generic template language, placeholders, robotic wording, unsupported claims and writer instructions.
